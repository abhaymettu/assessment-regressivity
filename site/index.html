Property assessment · Dane County, Wisconsin · 2025 roll

Six Wisconsin assessing offices copied 2024 sale prices onto the 2025 roll, and the state statistic that certifies them cannot see it

A ratio study of 169,025 residential parcels and 13,485 arms-length sales, built only from records Wisconsin already publishes. Two findings: a clerical practice that creates unequal assessments between neighbours, and a roll that is regressive underneath it.

Every figure on this page is computed at build time by the scripts in the repository. Nothing is typed in twice. The pipeline reproduces the published Cook County sales ratio study before it is pointed at Wisconsin.

01 · the finding the project did not set out to make

39.6% of sales that closed before the lien date carry an assessed value equal to the sale price, to the dollar

For sales that closed after it, 1.0%. The lien date supplies a control group for free: a sale in March 2025 cannot have informed a roll that was already fixed on 1 January. The share by month falls off a cliff exactly there, from 41.6% in December 2024 to 6.0% in January to 0.1% by May, and the January residue is the lag between closing and recording.

Red is the period the assessor was setting the roll, blue is after it closed. Each bar is the share of that month's arms-length residential sales whose 2025 assessed value equals the sale price within one dollar.

This is sales chasing, and it matters twice. Sold parcels were corrected to market while their unsold neighbours were not, so two identical houses now carry different assessments according to which one happened to change hands. And it corrupts the measurement: any ratio study drawing on 2024 sales grades the assessor using the very parcels the assessor already copied, and returns a cleaner verdict than the roll deserves. Everything below is measured on the 5,821 chase-free sales only.

02 · a practice, not a pipeline

Nothing occupies the 68 points between the offices that chase and the offices that do not

If sale prices were being written into the parcel layer by some county or state process, every municipality would chase at about the same rate and this would be a finding about a data pipeline. Across the 18 Dane County municipalities with enough pre-lien sales to measure, 6 sit at 69% or above and 12 sit at 0.9% or below. It is not a gradient. It is a practice an assessing office either uses or does not.

Five counties, not one. Every municipality above 50% did an interim market update in 2025, and every one of those is either Accurate Appraisal LLC or the City of Madison's in-house office. Hover any dot for the municipality, its assessor and its sample size.

03 · opportunity and choice

Only an interim market update lets an assessor set new values parcel by parcel, and inside that window one firm copies the sale price 9 times out of 9

Wisconsin municipalities file an assessment type each year, and the Department of Revenue publishes it. A municipality on maintenance is not touching individual parcels at all, so it cannot chase and is not evidence either way. That splits the question into an opportunity and a choice, and the data separates them completely.

The wording here is deliberately narrow. What is measured is an exact-match count between two public numbers. It describes a practice and says nothing about intent, and the arithmetic is reproducible by anyone with the same two public files.

04 · the roll underneath

The cheapest tenth of Dane County homes is assessed at 15.2% more of its sale price than the priciest tenth

Measured only on the 5,821 sales the assessor could not have chased, with prices restated to the lien date. The median ratio passes. Dispersion and the price-related differential do not, and the aggregate ratio the state certifies on, 0.860, takes the same value whether the burden is spread evenly or concentrated on the cheapest homes.

05 · the statistic that changes its mind

PRB, the measure IAAO added to be robust, reports a progressive roll on data built to be regressive, once dispersion passes about 22

On this roll PRB says progressive at +0.024 while the decile table, the price-related differential and a direct regression all say regressive. PRB regresses ratio deviation on a value proxy built partly from the assessed value it is testing, so a parcel assessed too high gets both a high y and a high x, and the contamination grows with the dispersion.

Every simulated roll here is regressive by construction: log ratio falls exactly 0.118 per doubling of price, with only the assessment noise dialled up. PRB slides from -0.129 at COD 6 to +0.125 at COD 30. A proxy built from price alone and the direct slope stay flat throughout.

At this county's COD of 18.4 the mechanism accounts for most of the collapse in magnitude and not for the whole change of sign, and what closes the remaining gap is not settled here. The operational conclusion does not depend on settling it: PRB is not a safe arbiter at this dispersion, so the direct slope is reported beside it everywhere in this study.

06 · the control, and the trap inside it

Adding house characteristics makes the estimate 3.7 times larger and tells you nothing

The obvious objection to the previous slide is that cheap and expensive homes are different homes. The City of Madison publishes its assessor's own inputs, so 2,405 of its chase-free sales carry year built, living area, bedrooms, baths, style, basement, air conditioning, lot size and the office's own area code across 275 assessment areas. Controlling for them drives the coefficient from -0.1130 to -0.4132, which looks like a much stronger finding and is arithmetic.

The dependent variable is log(assessed) minus log(price) and the regressor is log2(price), so price sits on both sides. Controls that predict assessed value strip out the part of the left side that is not price. In the limit the coefficient goes to minus the natural log of two, -0.6931, however fair the roll is. That is not asserted here, it is measured: a synthetic roll is built on which the assessor is neutral by construction, and every specification is run against it.

The fully controlled null lands on -0.6931 to four decimals. The specification that works replaces realised sale price with predicted price, fitted from the characteristics and the assessment area, so nothing from the individual transaction enters the regressor. It returns +0.0000 on the neutral roll, by construction rather than by luck, and -0.0626 with a standard error of 0.0077 on the real one.

So the answer is yes, with a haircut. About 45% of Madison's apparent regressivity was the composition of its housing stock. What is left is 55% of the uncontrolled figure, 1.3 times IAAO's neutral band of plus or minus 0.05, and 8 standard errors from zero. It is two houses of the same age, size, style and assessment neighbourhood being assessed at different fractions of what they are worth.

One sensitivity, stated rather than buried. The Madison sales that fail to join the characteristics layer move the uncontrolled slope from -0.0502 on all of them to -0.1130 on the ones that join. They are parcels the state layer codes as class-1 residential but Madison holds no house record for: apartment buildings, assemblies, and one parcel assessed at 0.14 of a $3.4m sale.

07 · does any of this pipeline work

The same code, pointed at Cook County, lands on the published study including the year PRB changes sign

A ratio study that has never been checked against anyone else's is a self-consistent artifact. The Center for Municipal Finance at the University of Chicago publishes one for Cook County covering 2015 to 2019, with sample size, COD, PRD and PRB for each year and a median ratio for each of ten price deciles. That is 35 numbers to miss. The statistics below are imported from the same module that judges Dane County, not reimplemented.

Matching a sign change on the same year is not. The decile gradient reproduces as shape: the cheapest tenth of Cook County homes is assessed at 27.2% more of sale price here against 28.4% published.

The published report does not say which assessment stage it read or whether it applied the Assessor's own sale flags, and the Assessor has since backfilled the sales file from MyDec, so today's extract carries roughly 20% more conveyances for these years. The specification is therefore swept across twelve combinations and the whole sweep is printed by the script rather than hidden. That is fitting to the target and is labelled as such. The decile gradient lands between 27% and 29% under all twelve, so the finding does not depend on the choice.

08 · what this does and does not say

Two separate problems, and correcting the first would not fix the second

What the data supports

  • Six Dane County offices assessed a large majority of pre-lien sales at exactly the sale price, and twelve assessed effectively none. The split is not a gradient.
  • Every chasing municipality across five counties did an interim market update, which is the only assessment type where copying a sale price is possible at all.
  • The roll is regressive on chase-free sales in all 11 municipalities with 100 or more of them, with a median slope of -0.159.
  • In Madison the regressivity survives the assessor's own house characteristics and neighbourhood codes at -0.0626 per doubling.

What it does not

  • Nothing here establishes why any office assesses the way it does. The measurement is an exact-match count between two public numbers.
  • The two findings are close to independent. Chasing rate and regressivity slope correlate at r = -0.27 across the municipalities measurable on both, so stopping the chasing would not fix the regressivity.
  • Madison, the heaviest chaser by volume, is the least regressive jurisdiction in the county once its chased sales are removed.
  • Five of the roughly twenty counties the largest contractor works in have been tested. The rest are open.

Method, in one paragraph

Sales come from Wisconsin Real Estate Transfer Returns, filtered to the state's own arms-length coding, residential property types and prices above $1,000. Assessments come from the WI DOA statewide parcel layer, class 1 only, and join to sales at 91.1% on parcel number. Because assessments are fixed at the lien date and sales run either side of it, the drift of log ratio against sale date estimates market movement and every price is restated to 1 January 2025 before any ratio is used. Fitting that trend on all sales returns 14.3% annual growth, on chase-free sales 6.0%; the first figure is an artifact of the clerical practice on slide 01 rather than the housing market, which is the second reason the chased sales have to come out.

A kill criterion was set before the data was pulled: fewer than 1,500 clean arms-length residential sales joining to parcels and the study moves to a county with better records rather than quietly dropping resolution. It returned 5,821.
